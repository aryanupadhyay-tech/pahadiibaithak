The problem on the trail

India's trekking boom runs on tired mules and gas cylinders.

“Every plate of Maggi at 4,000 metres was carried there by an animal. The wrapper usually stays behind.”Field note, Hampta Pass circuit

  1. Cruelty in the supply chain

    Raw rations, water and LPG cylinders are carried up on mules and horses. On steep, icy sections the animals are overloaded and overworked.

  2. Waste in a fragile ecosystem

    Plastic wrappers, foil packs and leftover food pile up at campsites. Hardly anyone takes litter back down the mountain.

  3. Food is costly and often unsafe

    Hauling heavy supplies makes every meal expensive. Water boils at a lower temperature up there, so food is often undercooked.

  4. No safety net once you're off-grid

    Past the last village there's no signal and no check-in point. If something goes wrong, nobody knows where you are.

  5. Few jobs for local youth

    The money from trekking mostly goes to operators in the cities. Young people in mountain villages get little of it.

01 · The product

A trail menu that weighs almost nothing.

The meals are high-energy Indian food, freeze-dried at base camp. You just add water from the stream. Each pouch heats itself, and the bowl and spoon are made of baked millet, so you can eat them too.

02 · Stall-in-a-Box

A whole restaurant that folds into a backpack frame.

The kiosk is modular and powers itself. Two people can put it up in under an hour, and it folds back down when the trekking season ends, so nothing is left on the mountain.

04 · The zero-waste loop

We pay trekkers to bring their trash back.

Every meal carries a ₹50 deposit. Return the empty wrapper at any stall and you get the money back in cash or by UPI, plus a stamp in your Trekker's Passport. Three stamps get you a free mountain chai.

07 · Working proof of concept

Phase 1 runs on WhatsApp, a pulley and real food.

You don't need custom drones to prove the model works. Our first version uses technology that's already available and cheap: a WhatsApp bot for orders and check-ins, gravity pulleys like the ones Himachal apple farmers use, and ready-made dehydrated meals.

  • Trekkers order and pre-book meals on WhatsApp before they lose signal.
  • Every check-in is logged, so the base camp knows who is on the trail and where.
  • Wrapper refunds are paid by UPI as soon as the stall scans the pouch.

08 · Zero-capital micro-franchise

Villagers open a stall without paying anything up front.

We give the Stall-in-a-Box to local partners for free. In the first season we take a larger share of sales to recover the hardware cost. After that, the split moves toward the partner.

Pahadi Baithak 55%
Village partner 45%

Proposed split. In season 1, a stall selling about 420 meals a week earns its partner roughly ₹28,200 a month. That's from day one, with nothing invested.

Step 01

Recruit

We sign up families and youth groups from villages near the trail, such as Prini, Sethan and Jagatsukh.

Step 02

Train

Partners get a week of training in hygiene, UPI payments, first aid and the SOS beacon.

Step 03

Supply

Standardised pouches arrive by porter or ropeway. The forecast model sets how much each stall gets.

Step 04

Earn

Partners run the stall under our brand on a profit-sharing basis, with income paid out weekly.

09 · Why this idea

Built to the INNOVEX brief: original, feasible and with real impact.

Ethical

The whole supply chain is free of animal cruelty. No mule or horse carries a single gram.

Eco-friendly

No food waste, fully biodegradable packaging, edible cutlery and solar-powered stalls.

Social

Direct jobs for porters and a business of their own for villagers, with no capital needed.

Safety

The food network also works as the first offline check-in and SOS grid on the trail.

10 · Roadmap

Start simple now. Get smarter as we grow.

We're first-year students, and the plan grows with what we learn in our B.Tech CSE (AI) programme.

Phase 1 · Right now

Prove it works

  • WhatsApp bot for orders and check-ins
  • Basic gravity pulleys and local porters
  • Ready-made dehydrated meals
  • ₹50 deposit and paper passports
  • 1 pilot stall on the Hampta Pass route
Phase 2 · Years 2–3

Franchise the trail

  • 6 Stall-in-a-Box kiosks run by village partners
  • Our own branded freeze-dried menu
  • Self-heating pouches and edible millet bowls
  • Offline intranet and satellite SOS beacons
Phase 3 · Years 3–4

Intelligent supply

  • AI demand forecasting from weather, permit and pass data
  • Custom heavy-payload cargo drones
  • Solar ropeways at the steepest sections
  • Expansion to other trails: Kedarkantha, Valley of Flowers, Sandakphu
